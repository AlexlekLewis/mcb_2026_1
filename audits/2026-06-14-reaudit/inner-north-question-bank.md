**Accuracy anchors:**
- West/afternoon sun: double-cell honeycomb and light-coloured blockout rollers are the documented interior plays; light colours reflect rather than absorb. (External shading does more, but interior furnishings are MCB's lane.)
- Child safety is a **real regulated topic**: AU corded-window-covering standard — no loop 220mm+ within 1.6m of the floor; ACCC recommends cordless/motorised under-6. Cordless, motorised and shutters are the cord-free answers. This is a credibility/E-E-A-T win to state accurately.
- Sheers: day privacy yes, **night privacy no** once interior lights are on — the honest answer is "layer with blockout." State it straight; it builds trust and sells the double-track.
- Condensation: be careful — heavy coverings can trap cold air against single glazing and *worsen* surface condensation/mould behind blockout if there's no airflow. Honest framing ("coverings manage comfort, but ventilation and glazing fix condensation") is more credible than over-claiming.

# 8–12 genuinely-local inner-north content angles
Specific, accurate hooks to REPLACE the wrong "Eltham mud-brick / bushland" copy currently mis-served on these pages. Each is verifiable and ties a local truth to a window-furnishing decision.

1. **Double-hung sash windows on Victorian/Edwardian terraces** (Fitzroy, Carlton, Clifton Hill, Northcote) — dress the two sashes independently: tier-on-tier shutters or a sheer-plus-blockout double track give street privacy on the lower pane and light up top. The everyday inner-north window problem, solved.

2. **Narrow frontages on tight one-way streets** (Fitzroy, Collingwood, Brunswick, Clifton Hill) — front rooms sit metres from the footpath and parked cars. Day-privacy sheers that still pull light, layered with blockout for night, beat a bare blind on a street where neighbours and passers-by are close.

3. **Heritage-overlay streetscapes** (Carlton, Fitzroy, and Northcote/Thornbury precincts around High St & St Georges Rd) — owners worry about what they're "allowed" to do. The honest hook: custom furnishings that sit inside the existing reveal, fit the original timber, and come out cleanly are the reversible, non-invasive approach overlay homes call for. (Point them to their council overlay; we handle the fit.)

4. **Heavy concentration of Californian bungalows** (Thornbury and Northcote especially — among the densest in the inner north) — deep timber reveals and wide, low window proportions that off-the-shelf sizes simply don't fit. The custom-measure hook is literally built into the housing stock.

5. **Weatherboard workers' cottages, 100+ years old** (Northcote, Thornbury, Brunswick, Preston) — original, slightly out-of-square openings where a measured, made-to-fit blind or curtain sits properly and a generic one gapes and lets light leak. False economy of "near enough" sizing lands hard here.

6. **High ceilings + single glazing = winter heat loss** (period terraces and Edwardians across the inner north) — tall rooms bleed heat through single-glazed sashes. Floor-to-ceiling lined drapes and honeycomb blinds are the thermal-comfort play for the genuinely cold inner-north winter night, without touching the heritage glazing.

7. **West-facing brick terraces baking from 2–6pm** — solid double-brick walls hold afternoon heat and west-facing front or rear rooms cop the worst of it. Double-cell honeycomb and light-coloured blockout rollers are the interior answer to the Preston-radius summer afternoon.

8. **Renter-heavy pockets** (Brunswick, Fitzroy, Collingwood, parts of Preston/Reservoir) — share-house and rental demand for privacy and blackout without drilling or losing the bond. No-drill, clip-in and tension options are a real, searched need; a measure-and-quote that respects a rental is a differentiator.

9. **Renovators restoring period homes** (Northcote, Thornbury bungalow conversions are a documented trend) — mid-reno owners choosing furnishings that read period-sympathetic from the street but perform like modern blockout/thermal inside. Timber Venetians and Romans that echo original joinery are the bridge.

10. **Nurseries and young families in family-forming inner-north suburbs** (Preston, Reservoir, Ivanhoe, Heidelberg) — cordless/child-safe is both a regulated requirement and a parent's first question; blackout for naps in a bright, west-exposed cottage room is the practical follow-on.

11. **Bay windows on Italianate/Victorian frontages** (Carlton, Fitzroy, Northcote) — angled bays are an awkward shape for one flat blind; tier-on-tier shutters or individually-fitted panels handle the geometry while keeping the period proportion.

12. **Street-facing front rooms used as bedrooms/studies** (terraces with no front setback) — the classic inner-north "the front room is right on the street" problem: needs full privacy at desk-height and night, light by day. Layered treatment is the standard, accurate recommendation.

# Honesty note — search-volume reality

**Genuinely high Melbourne-level search volume (build dedicated guide + product copy, expect to rank for the query itself):**
- All of **Theme 1 (cost/pricing)** — "how much do blinds/curtains/plantation shutters cost" is the single most-contested query set; every major Melbourne retailer targets it. Highest priority.
- **Theme 4 "free in-home measure and quote [suburb]"** and "do you come to me" — strong commercial intent, near-universal competitor landing pages, converts.
- **Theme 5 heat/west-sun** ("best blinds to keep heat out") and **privacy/sheers at night** — solid, recurring demand (retailer guides + active forum threads).
- **Theme 2** blinds-vs-curtains, sheer-vs-blockout, honeycomb-for-heat — steady evergreen informational volume.
- **Theme 6** "are motorised blinds worth it" and "how long do blinds last" — real, forum-validated decision queries.
- **Child-safety / cordless / nursery** (Theme 5) — real volume AND an E-E-A-T credibility win (regulated topic).

**Local-context angles with LOW direct search volume — but high relevance for prose + AEO (don't expect to rank for the exact phrase; use them to make pages unique, accurate, and citable by AI answer engines):**
- Most of **Theme 3's hyper-specific phrasings** — "blinds for double-hung sash windows," "heritage-overlay window coverings," "Californian bungalow window treatments," "tier-on-tier for bay windows." Few people type these verbatim, but they're exactly the substance that makes a suburb page genuinely about *that* suburb's homes, and they're the kind of specific, factual passage that LLM answer engines lift and cite.
- The **suburb-by-suburb housing-stock hooks** (angles 1–12) have little standalone keyword volume each, but collectively they replace generic/wrong copy with accurate local detail — which lifts topical authority, dwell time, and AI-citation odds for the whole location-page set.
- **Condensation/mould** is real volume but a *thin* fit for a furnishings business — cover it honestly and briefly (coverings manage comfort; ventilation/glazing fix condensation) rather than chasing the keyword, or you'll over-claim and lose trust.

**Bottom line:** lead each location page with the high-volume cost + measure + heat/privacy questions to earn the click, then differentiate and earn AI citations with the accurate inner-north housing-stock angles.
